Scatter Meter: Legit Ba?

By DemoLang Editorial Team · Updated 2026-11-12

Short answer

A scatter meter (also called a slot meter or jackpot meter) is a website that shows a percentage or signal for each slot game and suggests which one is about to give scatters. It cannot predict your next spin. Every spin is decided by the game's random number generator, separately from the spins before it, and even the best-known meter site says in its own disclaimer that no reading guarantees a win. Many meter sites earn money when you sign up to a casino through their links.

Ano ang ipinapakita ng scatter meter

Typical meter sites list hundreds of games, each with a number like "84%", colored bars, up or down arrows, and columns for the last 10 minutes, 1 hour or 6 hours. Some add a "daily scatter level" or a 5-level win signal. The idea they sell: pick the game whose meter is high, or wait for the right time, and scatters will come.

On TikTok and Facebook the same idea goes by names like meter base scatter, metrohan or time pattern.

Bakit hindi nito kayang hulaan ang panalo

1. Bawat spin ay hiwalay

Licensed slots use a tested random number generator (RNG). The GLI-11 testing standard requires that outcomes are not influenced by anything other than the RNG, that one result gives no information about the next, and that a game does not adjust its chances based on what it paid before. So a game that has not given scatters for an hour is not "due", and a game that just paid is not "cold".

2. Ang tsansa ay pareho kahit ano ang nangyari dati

Say a game gives free spins about once every 150 spins on average. The chance that they come in your next 150 spins is about 63%, whether the last 500 spins had ten bonuses or none. A meter that looks at the past does not change that number.

3. Ang RTP ay para sa milyon-milyong spin

A game's RTP, like 96.5%, describes the long-run average over millions of spins by all players. It is not a tank that fills up and empties. A meter saying a game is "at 84%" does not mean it has to pay back the rest to you.

4. Hindi mo ma-check ang data nila

Meter sites do not publish how their percentages are calculated. One of the biggest says it reads "publicly available" data from game providers and, on the same page, that its feed costs over $60,000 a year. Either way, its own footer says it "does not guarantee wins or influence actual slot game outcomes", and that the result of every spin is decided by the game's RNG.

Paano kumikita ang mga meter site

The same About page says the tool is supported through "casino partner relationships": when a visitor clicks Play Now and joins a platform, the site earns a referral. That is the business model. A free meter that sends you to a casino is advertising, even when it looks like data.
